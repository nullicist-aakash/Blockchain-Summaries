Bitcoin: A Technical Introduction - Volume I

Chapter 3 Blockchain vs Bitcoin

3.1 How Bitcoin works on surface

Bitcoin is basically a ledger system. In simple terms, a ledger keeps record of transactions. It is supposed to track the balances of individual people, track the amount of money they send each other, etc. Bitcoin is just like that. As per the problem statement of Bitcoin paper by Satoshi Nakamoto, the main goal of Bitcoin is to track money and make sure it is there where it is claimed.

Why do we need a ledger at all? Isn’t the money different from ledger? Isn’t the ledger just a bookkeeping but the actual money operates independent of it? True. Paper money don’t need a ledger to work. But what about money in bank accounts? When we send money to some other account, what does it mean to “send” money? Are there physical lockers in banks in which they transfer cash from one locker to another? Think of thousands of UPI transactions that occur in a day in India. Are there some people or some machine sitting in bank which carefully transfers paper cash from one “locker” to another? Or, bank just use an internal ledger where they simply add and subtract numbers? Think about it deeply before proceeding further. If we don’t have the idea of importance of a ledger, we can’t possibly understand the importance of properties it should have.

3.1.1 Requirements of a ledger

How a ledger fits in this big picture? Say person \(A\) sends \(x\) amount of money to person \(B\). A ledger is supposed to keep track of this in a legitimate manner. A ledger is supposed to do something in the lines of:

  1. Check if the “address” of person \(A\) is valid, is registered in the system.
  2. Check if the “address” of person \(B\) is valid, and is registered in the system.
  3. Check if person \(A\) have sufficient balance to send money to person \(B\).
  4. Verify that person who is sending money from account \(A\), is actually person \(A\).
  5. Update balance of individual people by subtracting \(x\) amount from balance of person \(A\) and adding \(x\) amount in the balance of person \(B\).
  6. Stores the time of this transaction.
  7. Once a transaction is made, its entry should not be deleted!
  8. Once a transaction is made, no one should be able to change any entry! Not a single digit!

These are the basic requirements. We will uncover many more as we read about Bitcoin, but our major goal is to achieve above and prove that it is possible to achieve it using technology, not via a “trust” in some person or organisation. All this should be guaranteed by the “underlying” system, so that no one can use a loophole, not even the banks or government.

One thing is missing in above ledger requirements. Can you guess? It is a very critical requirement. We want no single authority on the ledger! We want that no one owns that ledger. What we mean is that filling entries in the ledger should not be the responsibility of a single entity or organisation. This statement seems vague, what do we actually mean by it?

3.1.2 Who stores the ledger?

Say some “entity” is “managing” this ledger. By managing, we refer to storing the records of this ledger and updating the entries in it, etc. This is what banks actually do. We don’t want this ledger “management” in the hands of a single person. That will mean the “manager” of this ledger have the absolute power. The “manager” can delete the ledger at any time, can show partial information to some users and some information to others. This “manager” can block some users from even registering in the system, can charge money to the users of this ledger, etc. We don’t want any of this. The core problem is that some “entity” is owning the “management” of the ledger.

What Bitcoin does is it stores this ledger with every person who is using Bitcoin! Basically, every Bitcoin user have the exact copy of this ledger on their system, so that no single entity is incharge of “managing” the ledger. This is what we were doing in previous chapter while getting our hands on initial transactions. We were downloading this ledger in our system, and the Bitcoin ledger is touching \(500\) gb of storage. This includes each and every transaction from the beginning of Bitcoin and every person can see the transactions on their device without depending on “others” for reliability of data. We stopped the process in between to get our hands on initial \(128\) mb of data for processing.

In Bitcoin, say two person are exchanging money with each other. They will tell others in the world that they did the transaction and send the transaction details to everyone in the world.

3.1.3 Consequences of ledger storage

At this point, you should think about the implications of this decision. While it eliminates the “trust” problems of our current financial system, it introduces a lot of problems for the system to work! What stops a person from receiving \(200\) dollars in a transaction but claim they only received \(100\) dollars? Or, what stops a person from sending \(100\) dollars but enter that they sent \(200\) dollars in the system. After all, both parties will broadcast their transaction details to everyone in the world, but whose record will be trusted? The one sending money or the one receiving money?

Also, assuming that everyone is operating honestly (We can’t assume this at all, but for the sake of simplicity, assume this for once), there are thousands of transactions happening per second in the world. How the users are supposed to update their entries? Say we have the following scenario: 1. Person \(A\) send \(100\) dollars to person \(B\). This increases the balance of person \(B\) to \(300\) dollars. Both tell the world about this. 2. Meanwhile, person \(C\) sends \(50\) dollars to person \(B\) and this information reaches \(B\) just after \(A\). So, the ledger of \(B\) will show that the balance first increases from \(200\) to \(300\), and then \(300\) to \(350\).

See the problem here? What about the balance record of \(B\) that is stored in ledger of \(C\)? After all, everyone is storing the records of every transactions in their copy of ledger. Since \(C\) is sending \(50\) dollars to \(B\), local ledger of \(C\) will update that balance of \(B\) is increased from \(200\) to \(250\). After some time, the announcement of \(A\) and \(B\) comes to \(C\) (due to large distance of \(C\) from both of them) which increased the balance of \(B\) by 100 and new balance of \(B\) is \(300\)!! This makes the entries of \(C\) inconsistent, effectively making ledger prone to errors. How Bitcoin solves this problem? We will cover this in Volume two in detail, but remember that the problem here is storing the balance. Bitcoin doesn’t store the balance of individuals, at all. How does it work, as is already mentioned, will be covered in future volume. This volume focuses on some other core problem.

3.1.4 Solution to the problems

We will cover all the problems one by one in detail. Some problems are covered in this volume and some in future volumes of this book. Some problems are inherent to malicious people doing transaction, some are dependent on how we are keeping track of transactions. There are many more technical problems that needs to be solved. Consider point 4 of ledger requirements mentioned above. How do we do that by code? There are no identities of people on internet. They can come and go from any device, at any point in time. They can be offline for a year from the system while their account is still active. We need to make sure that the actual owner of account is operating the account assigned to him, not the account of someone else.

We will study Blockchain in this volume which covers half of the problems and will work on transaction data in future.

3.2 Problems solved by blockchain

One thing that need to be cleared in the beginning: Blockchain is different technology than the Bitcoin or any other cryptocurrency! Blockchain provides us what we need in a ledger system: Unable to delete something once it is there in the system, and unable to change anything once it is there in the system. No matter how carefully we do some transaction, perform various checks; if our records can be changed by any chance in future, the system is flawed. Someone can basically go deep in the history and change the amount of money transferred to them. This can be mitigated if the records are stored on every device, as the ledger is stored on various devices. But we need something more. First, let’s see the problem in verifying the data across devices if one thinks that some entry is changed. Who in the world ask every computer on earth to check the entry they have about a specific transaction? Computers are basically connected with nearby 2-3 devices (We will cover the networking in future volume, but take this number to be a dummy number). They can ask for data in these devices and those will provide the data.

3.2.1 Changing data in existing ledger

Assuming that data can be changed in future and a person can’t verify if the entry is changed, what the person will do? Ask their neighbors! Ask them what is the entry for a specific transaction. If they say that the entry matches with the one we are checking, we are bound to think that the entry is correct. Can you think of a problem in this approach? The one which can be exploited to control the whole system to their advantage?

Let’s pay we are in debt of someone and we have to pay a million dollar to get out of debt. We already paid some money to them but are unable to pay the remaining balance. Only if there is something we can do to come out of debt in the system… Let’s pay some money to hackers and tell them to infect a group of connected devices to the person to whom we have to pay the money! Tell the hackers to change the entry of specific transactions to make it look like that the debt is already paid. Not only change the entry to the specific person, but the ones too who are connected to that person. Just to be safe, hack their neighbor devices too. You are done. You are finally debt free by paying some money to hackers!

Wait… that person can check the balances after each transaction, right? Not really. Remember that Bitcoin doesn’t store the net balance after each transaction. We discussed a case when that can cause problems.

So there you have it. And this is a huge problem, as we can change anything in storage, any time. There are really no checks in the system to stop you from making changes when you are literally the owner of your copy of ledger! No admin rights required, just open the file and change the data. If you can change anything, what stops a hacker from changing it? There is a problem we need to solve. Only if there is a solution to this problem… Only if we can have some kind of data that can’t be changed at all when that data is literally stored on disks/SSDs that can be edited anytime… Did we say that Bitcoin prices are going through the roof? So, there is a solution to this? Really?

3.2.2 Detect changes in data

This problem is solved by: Blockchain. And there you have it, the difference between Bitcoin and Blockchain. While the Bitcoin is a system of ledger, Blockchain provides us the feature of permanent storage. Blockchain gives us power to cast something in stone that can’t be changed, at all, in the disks that can be changed anytime. Blockchain gives us the power to have trust in Blockchain. It gives you guarantee that no one can change any data in the system, once it is entered by someone. We don’t have to worry about any hacker or ill intent person to take benefit of anyone. And we don’t need any special software to implement it, it is the inherent property of the system in which Bitcoin data is stored. And guess what, this is completely independent of how cryptocurrencies are used. This means, that the technology literally tells us if even a single bit is changed.

3.2.3 Blockchain Requirements

And now you know: The idea of what this volume is all about. The idea of what Blockchain is all about. We don’t have any idea how it is implemented, but we have concrete idea of what we need. We need something to:

  1. Add entries in the system and make sure they are not deleted in future.
  2. Once something is there in the system, it can’t be changed at all. Literally!

If someone changes something, the system simply detects it and informs that something fishy is going on. And since data is stored on various computers, and computers can tell if their data is changed, we can literally recover the system if there is atleast one computer which is not infected. And the recovery is not based on how many computers have majority of same data. Even if 1 billion devices are infected, they can simply tell their data is wrong. Someone changed the data. And there is one device, which reports that its data is not changed, which everyone can verify, all 1 billion devices will accept that device data as genuine, download the copy of that device and the system will start working again.

So, what did we cover in this lesson? Here are the questions you should be able to answer:

  • What is the importance of a ledger in digital currencies? Why digital currencies can’t work without some kind of a ledger?
  • What are the basic requirements of a company/entity/bank owned ledger? What minimal information should it store?
  • Why we don’t want the ledger to be stored at a single company or entity? Why do we need to store ledger in every device that participate in transactions for the Bitcoin to work?
  • What are the obvious issues of doing transactions with ledger that is stored on each device?
  • Why we don’t want to store “net balance” in distributed ledger after each transaction?
  • What are the storage requirements of a ledger? Why do we need the storage data to be fixed, once it is written?
  • What hacks/exploits can one person use to play with the system if the storage was not fixed?
  • How can the Blockchain system recover if the data of particular device is corrupted/modified/deleted?
  • What problem does the Blockchain technology solve, and what is the role of Blockchain in the functioning of Bitcoin?

And most importantly

  • What are the feature requirements from a Blockchain technology?
